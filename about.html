About me.

In the Nicomachean Ethics, Aristotle separated technē, the knowledge of how to make a thing, from phronēsis, the judgment of what is worth making. They are different competencies, and a civilization can be excellent at one while being poor at the other. My current motivation lies in developing both competencies. “Worth making” is never a general question — worth it for whom, decided by whom, checked by whom — and answering each part requires a different set of skills.

At Carnegie Mellon, I study computer science with concentrations in machine learning, mathematics, and philosophy. This combination helps me build intuition for the buildable, formal, and normative parts of the problems I care about. The subtopics I am currently most interested in are formal epistemology, metaheuristic optimization, and mechanistic interpretability. Though seemingly different, all three come down to how systems search for solutions and how we make sense of their reasoning.

My current work is split across a few places. I do metaheuristic optimization research at CMU's Biorobotics Lab, and spent this summer on alignment and prompt optimization at Autonomize AI. I also do fieldbuilding and outreach with the Carnegie AI Safety Initiative, and spend time on the policy side of AI governance. More on each in projects.

Outside of academics, I enjoy backpacking, motorsports, poker, and writing on my Substack.

If any of this is something you think about too, I would like to hear from you: nirvikpande@gmail.com.
