RLMOpt: Adaptive Prompt Optimization via Recursive Language Models

S. Bangalore Satheesha, N. Pande, D. Duddempudi, B. Dandala · August 2026

Prompt search driven by a recursive language model agent rather than a fixed optimizer. It beats GEPA, the current state of the art, on all four benchmarks and on 9 of 11 matched runs — while using fewer rollouts and producing prompts 27–79% the size of GEPA's. Targetting ICLR 2027.

Range Extension with Supernormals for Mixed-Precision 8-bit DNN Training

S. W. Pun, B. Bao, S.-I. Filip, G. Lemieux, J. V. Kim, N. Misyats, N. Pande, V. Ravain, R. Sherrick · May 2025

Supernormal 8-bit encodings that replace subnormals, extending range from 33 to 36 binades and cutting multiply-accumulate area while holding accuracy comparable to binary32. The result makes 8-bit training viable on cheaper hardware. Published at IEEE ARITH 2025.

Model alignment for prior authorization

Autonomize AI · Machine Learning Engineer Intern · May – August 2026

Built the company's first RL-based alignment pipeline for an AI-driven prior authorization system, raising clinical decision accuracy from 87% to 94% over the heuristic baseline it replaced.

The alignment objective was Kahneman–Tversky Optimization: a prospect-theoretic utility function that trains directly on binary accept/reject signals, which removes the dependence on paired preference data that made the alternative approaches impractical here.

Research

Multi-agent path planning in continuous space

CMU Biorobotics Lab · September 2025 – present

Scalable multi-agent path planning with collision avoidance, for systems where agents visit multiple targets and discretising the space into a grid throws away too much.

I implemented SI-RRT* in C++ as the low-level planner, combining safe interval dynamic obstacle avoidance with a constraint table so the planner supports LNS-based replanning. The current work is a metaheuristic for the high-level multi-agent combinatorial path finding search, aiming at lower solution cost and better scaling than the LNS approach it replaces.
